# Video ideas: Psychology & human behavior (biases, habits, social dynamics)

## 1. Why You Refuse to Sleep When Exhausted
Explains Bedtime Revenge Procrastination, where people stay up late scrolling because it is the only part of the day they feel in total control over. It turns bedtime guilt into an eye-opening psychological revelation.

Hook (en): You are exhausted, but you won't sleep.
Why now: Relatable nightly habits drive massive virality and comment-section debate because viewers watch Shorts in bed while actively doing this exact behavior.
Keywords: bedtime procrastination, revenge bedtime procrastination psychology, why do i stay up late, sleep psychology hacks, habits and burnout
- Kroese et al. (2014) - Bedtime procrastination: introducing a new area of procrastination
- Sleep Foundation clinical review on Revenge Bedtime Procrastination

## 2. The Strange Trap of Announcing Your Goals
Reveals Social Reality Theory: telling someone about your big goal tricks your brain into feeling you already achieved it, slashing your real-world drive to finish it.

Hook (en): Never announce your goals to people.
Why now: Direct counter-intuitive advice challenges popular 'accountability partner' wisdom, instantly triggering retention and high share rates among self-improvement viewers.
Keywords: why telling goals ruins motivation, social reality theory, psychology of goal setting, dopamine trap goals, motivation psychology
- Gollwitzer et al. (2009) - When Intentions Go Public: Does Social Reality Widen the Intention-Behavior Gap?
- Kurt Lewin's Tensional States and Social Reality Framework

## 3. Why High Places Urge You to Jump
Demystifies the High Place Phenomenon (Call of the Void), explaining that the brief thought of jumping is actually your brain's rapid survival reflex misinterpreting a safety retreat as an urge.

Hook (en): Ever had the urge to jump?
Why now: Addresses a widespread, taboo intrusive thought that over 50% of people secretly experience, sparking high re-watch time and immense relief in the comments.
Keywords: call of the void explained, high place phenomenon, intrusive thoughts psychology, brain survival instincts, weird human behavior
- Hames et al. (2012) - An urge to jump affirms the urge to live: An empirical examination of the high place phenomenon
- Journal of Affective Disorders (Cognitive and sensory mismatch theory)
