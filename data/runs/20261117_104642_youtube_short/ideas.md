# Video ideas: Mind Hacks, AI Secrets, Bizarre Body Science & Hidden Power Dynamics

## 1. The Invisible Screen Mirroring Trap
Viewers are instructed to check a specific hidden indicator on their phone's notification bar or control center to see if an app is currently mirroring their screen. This triggers immediate paranoia and high engagement as users report their findings in the comments and share to warn others.

Hook (en): Your phone screen is being watched.
Why now: Privacy-focused 'fear-ware' content is currently peaking due to recent OS updates adding more transparency indicators. Videos showing how to 'check' for intruders have a massive Viewed-vs-Swiped-Away ratio because they demand an immediate physical check of the device.
Keywords: phone privacy, hidden settings, cyber security, smartphone hacks, screen recording
- Apple iOS Privacy Indicators (Green/Orange dots documentation)
- Android 12+ Privacy Dashboard and Camera/Mic access toggles

## 2. The 90-Minute Digital Alarm Glitch
An interactive challenge where viewers use their phone's calculator to find their 'perfect' wake-up time based on 90-minute sleep cycles. It uses the 'Live Challenge' archetype by forcing them to calculate and comment their ideal time for tomorrow morning.

Hook (en): Stop waking up tired.
Why now: Self-optimization and 'body glitches' are evergreen top-performers. Combining a mathematical 'hack' with a universal problem (waking up tired) guarantees high Average Percentage Viewed (APV) as they follow the steps.
Keywords: sleep hack, productivity secrets, circadian rhythm, brain optimization, biohacking
- Sleep Foundation: Understanding Sleep Cycles and REM
- Harvard Health: The Science of Sleep

## 3. The AI Keyboard Identity Test
A high-engagement interactive test where viewers let their phone's AI predictive text finish a specific sentence starting with 'I am going to...'. The bizarre results create a 'comment flood' of users sharing their funny or creepy AI-generated sentences.

Hook (en): Your phone's AI knows your secrets.
Why now: This uses the 'Interactive Challenge' archetype. Since every user gets a different result, it generates a massive comment-to-view ratio, which is the primary signal for the YouTube Shorts algorithm to push a video to a wider audience.
Keywords: predictive text, AI secrets, phone glitches, keyboard hacks, interactive challenge
- MIT Technology Review: How Predictive Text Models Work
- The Guardian: The Psychology of Autocomplete
