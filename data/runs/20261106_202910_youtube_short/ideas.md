# Video ideas: Psychology & human behavior (biases, habits, social dynamics)

## 1. The Pratfall Effect: Why Mistakes Make You Likable
A person acts perfectly versus a person who spills their coffee. The video explains that small blunders make highly competent people seem more human and approachable to others.

Hook (en): Stop trying to be perfect.
Hook (fr): Arrête de vouloir être parfait.
Why now: Authenticity is a high-performing trend on vertical video platforms; viewers feel relieved by the psychological proof that vulnerability builds trust.
Keywords: psychology, social hacks, likability, human behavior, charisme, psychologie, comportement
- Aronson, E., Willerman, B., & Floyd, J. (1966). The effect of a pratfall on increasing interpersonal attractiveness.

## 2. The IKEA Effect: Why You Love Your Own Work
Shows a person struggling with a simple DIY task or cooking a basic meal, then valuing it more than a professional version. Explains how labor leads to overvaluation.

Hook (en): You overvalue things you build.
Hook (fr): Tu surévalues ce que tu crées.
Why now: The 'IKEA effect' is a well-known but counter-intuitive bias that generates high engagement through relatability with DIY and creative hobbies.
Keywords: psychology, productivity, cognitive bias, psychology facts, biais cognitif, psychologie, cerveau
- Norton, M. I., Mochon, D., & Ariely, D. (2012). The IKEA effect: When labor leads to love.

## 3. The Peak-End Rule: How Memories Work
A long, boring event with one great moment is remembered better than a consistently 'okay' event. Shows that the brain only stores the peak and the end.

Hook (en): Your brain ignores the middle.
Hook (fr): Ton cerveau ignore le milieu.
Why now: This offers a 'life hack' perspective for travel and social planning, encouraging viewers to comment with their own stories of 'ruined' or 'saved' vacations.
Keywords: memory hacks, psychology, brain tricks, happiness, psychologie, mémoire, bien-être
- Kahneman, D., Fredrickson, B. L., Schreiber, C. A., & Redelmeier, D. A. (1993). When More Pain is Preferred to Less.
