# Video ideas: Mind Hacks, AI Secrets, Bizarre Body Science & Hidden Power Dynamics

## 1. The Paralyzed Pinky Glitch
An interactive physical glitch where the viewer tries to lift their pinky finger while their hand is in a specific flat position. It demonstrates a shared tendon between the ring and pinky fingers. Viewers are prompted to comment if they are the '1% who can move it'.

Hook (en): You can't move your pinky.
Why now: Physical 'glitch' videos have the highest VVSA because viewers must stop to attempt the movement. The physiological limitation of the 'extensor digiti minimi' creates an instant 'failure' loop that drives repeat views and high comment volume from people claiming they can beat it.
Keywords: body glitch, human biology hack, brain test, interactive challenge, physiology trick
- Anatomy of the extensor tendons of the hand
- Juncturae Tendinum physiological study

## 2. The 3-Second Silence Power Play
A social power dynamic trick where staying silent for 3 seconds after someone speaks forces them to over-explain and lose leverage. It uses the 'awkward silence' reflex to gain dominance in negotiations. Built-in trigger: 'Try this on your boss tomorrow and comment what happens.'

Hook (en): Silence is your strongest weapon.
Why now: Dark psychology and 'status' hacks are currently peaking on YouTube Shorts. This specific technique is highly actionable and creates curiosity about the psychological 'discomfort' it causes others, leading to high retention as the 'why' is explained.
Keywords: dark psychology, body language, negotiation tactics, social engineering, power dynamics
- Chris Voss - Never Split the Difference
- Journal of Social Psychology: The 4-second silence rule

## 3. The Impossible Number 7 Test
An interactive mental elimination test. Through a series of quick math and word associations, the viewer is guided to a 'random' number that 90% of people choose (the number 7). Includes a 'reveal' that forces a second watch to see where the trick happened.

Hook (en): I'll read your mind right now.
Why now: Interactive mind-reading remains the king of APV (Average Percentage Viewed) because the payoff happens at the very end, and viewers often re-watch to see if they were 'forced'. The 'Seven' bias is a well-documented psychological phenomenon (The Blue-Seven Phenomenon).
Keywords: mind reading, psychology test, interactive game, brain hack, magic trick
- The Blue-Seven Phenomenon research
- Simon Grondin - Psychology of Time and Numbers
