# Video ideas: Psychology & human behavior (biases, habits, social dynamics)

## 1. Why You Hate Your Recorded Voice
Explains why hearing a recording of yourself makes you cringe due to bone conduction vibrating your skull, making your internal voice sound deeper and richer than it actually is. It surprises viewers by revealing everyone else hears the 'cringe' version and thinks it is completely normal.

Hook (en): You don't sound like that.
Why now: Voice confrontation is a universally relatable everyday shock that triggers instant self-reflection and debate in the comments. Audio-perceptual phenomena reliably achieve high completion and share rates on YouTube Shorts because viewers immediately test it against their own experience.
Keywords: why you hate your voice, voice confrontation effect, bone conduction psychology, brain perception tricks, human behavior facts
- Holzman and Rouse (1966), 'The Voice Confrontation Effect', Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
- American Academy of Otolaryngology - Head and Neck Surgery (Bone conduction acoustic mechanisms)

## 2. Why Eating While Distracted Never Fills You Up
Unpacks why eating while scrolling or working leaves you craving snacks an hour later, revealing that your brain requires visual and sensory memory formation to encode fullness signals. It surprises viewers by showing satiety is just as much a cognitive memory process as a stomach reaction.

Hook (en): Stop eating at your desk.
Why now: Millions eat daily while staring at screens and struggle with mid-afternoon cravings without realizing the cognitive link. Health-adjacent behavioral insights consistently generate high save-rates and re-watches on short-form platforms.
Keywords: distracted eating psychology, memory and satiety, why you crave snacks, eating while scrolling, brain hunger cues
- Oldham-Cooper et al. (2011), 'Playing a computer game during lunch affects nourishment, memory for lunch, and later snack consumption', American Journal of Clinical Nutrition
- Higgs (2016), 'Cognitive influences on food intake: the role of memory', Physiology & Behavior

## 3. Why You Suddenly Clean When Stressed
Breaks down why high-stakes deadlines suddenly make organizing your desk or doing laundry intensely appealing, exposing it as structured procrastination where the brain swaps emotional threat for an easy dopaminergic win. Viewers feel validated yet called out by the psychological explanation.

Hook (en): Your clean room is hiding something.
Why now: Structured procrastination is an acute shared experience among students and remote professionals. Calling out this subconscious coping mechanism creates immediate emotional resonance and sparks active comment sections sharing personal habits.
Keywords: productive procrastination, why you clean when stressed, procrastination psychology, dopamine quick wins, brain avoidance behavior
- Perry, J. (1996), 'How to Procrastinate and Still Get Things Done', The Chronicle of Higher Education
- Sirois & Pychyl (2013), 'Procrastination and the Priority of Short-Term Mood Regulation', Social and Personality Psychology Compass
