# Video ideas: Psychology & human behavior (biases, habits, social dynamics)

## 1. Why You Can't Eat Without Watching Something
Explains sensory pairing and cue-conditioned dopamine loops: your brain has linked the act of chewing with passive video consumption, making quiet eating feel agonizingly under-stimulating. It reveals how decoupling them resets your satiety signals.

Hook (en): Stop eating until you find a video?
Why now: Hyper-relatable everyday modern habit with millions of comments on TikTok and Reels admitting they let their food get cold searching for a video. High comment engagement and immediate self-identification drive retention.
Keywords: dopamine pairing, eating while scrolling, habit loop psychology, distracted eating, sensory overstimulation
- American Journal of Clinical Nutrition - Distracted eating and food intake
- Journal of Behavioral Addictions - Conditioned smartphone checking and stimulus pairing

## 2. Why Doing Nothing Makes You Anxious
Examines the Default Mode Network and 'idleness aversion': when you finally sit down to relax, your brain switches to self-evaluative background processing, interpreting stillness as falling behind. Explains how purposeful active rest silences the false alarm.

Hook (en): Why does resting make you anxious?
Why now: Massive cultural pain point among younger demographics suffering from chronic burnout and toxic productivity. Triggers intense emotional resonance and high shares to friends who struggle to unwind.
Keywords: idleness aversion, default mode network, rest guilt, burnout psychology, productivity guilt
- Psychological Science - Idleness Aversion and the Need for Justification
- Nature Reviews Neuroscience - The Brain's Default Mode Network

## 3. Why 4 Seconds of Silence Feels Agonizing
Unpacks the 4-second conversational gap threshold: research shows that a pause longer than four seconds in casual dialogue triggers ancient social-exclusion fear circuitry in the amygdala, making silence feel three times longer than it actually is.

Hook (en): Silence feels awkward in four seconds.
Why now: Specific numbers in psychological hooks trigger intense curiosity. The video can physically demonstrate a calibrated 4-second pause, visually and auditorily gripping the viewer and proving the effect in real time.
Keywords: conversational silence, social rejection fear, awkward pause psychology, social dynamics, amygdala response
- Journal of Experimental Social Psychology - Disrupting the flow: How brief silences in conversation affect social connection
- Cognition - Timing and turn-taking in human conversation
