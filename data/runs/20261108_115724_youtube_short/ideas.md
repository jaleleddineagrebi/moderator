# Video ideas: Psychology & human behavior (biases, habits, social dynamics)

## 1. Why Switching Apps Makes You Tired
Explains the psychological cost of micro-task switching (attention residue). Moving between casual apps like messages, feeds, and browser tabs drains cognitive glucose even when you feel like you are just relaxing.

Hook (en): Resting on your phone drains you.
Hook (fr): Te reposer sur ton téléphone t'épuise.
Why now: Audiences constantly feel fatigued after digital downtime; this taps into universal daily frustration with a counter-intuitive cognitive explanation verified by organizational psychology.
Keywords: attention residue, mental fatigue, screen fatigue, multitasking psychology, fatigue mentale, résidu d'attention, cerveau fatigué
- Leroy, S. (2009). Why is it so hard to do my work? The challenge of attention residue when switching between work tasks. Organization Science.
- Mark, G. (2023). Attention Span: A Groundbreaking Way to Restore Balance, Happiness and Productivity.

## 2. Why Four Seconds of Silence Panics You
Unpacks conversational gap tolerance: conversational pauses stretching past four seconds trigger a primitive rejection alarm in human brains, inducing acute social anxiety.

Hook (en): Four seconds of silence panics you.
Hook (fr): Quatre secondes de silence te paniquent.
Why now: Universal conversational phenomenon with high relatability across both French and English conversational cultures, visually easy to depict using simple portraits and ticking cues.
Keywords: awkward silence psychology, conversational pause, social anxiety hack, silence conversation, malaise silence, psychologie sociale
- Koudenburg, N., Postmes, T., & Gordijn, E. H. (2011). Disrupting the flow: How brief silences in conversational flow affect social needs. Journal of Experimental Social Psychology.
- Kendrick, K. H., & Torreira, F. (2015). The timing and turn-taking of conversational sequences. Frontiers in Psychology.

## 3. Why Telling People Your Goals Kills Motivation
Reveals social reality theory: announcing an ambition tricks your brain into feeling premature reward and identity completion, stripping away the drive to do the actual work.

Hook (en): Never announce your goals out loud.
Hook (fr): Ne dis jamais tes objectifs.
Why now: Directly contradicts popular self-improvement advice about public accountability, driving strong comment debate and long watch-through rates.
Keywords: social reality theory, goal setting psychology, premature praise, motivation brain, garder ses objectifs secrets, motivation psychologie
- Gollwitzer, P. M., Sheeran, P., Michalski, V., & Seifert, A. E. (2009). When intentions go public: Does social reality inhibit goal striving? Psychological Science.
- Mahler, W. (1933). Ersatzhandlungen verschiedenen Realitatsgrades. Psychologische Forschung.
