# Video ideas: Psychology & human behavior (biases, habits, social dynamics)

## 1. Why Rereading Makes You Forget
It reveals that highlighting and rereading text creates a false sense of familiarity called 'the illusion of competence' rather than actual memory.

Hook (en): Stop rereading your notes right now.
Why now: Academic pressure and productivity culture are peaking on social media; videos debunking traditional study habits consistently go viral among students and professionals.
Keywords: learning psychology, memory hacks, study tips, brain behavior, illusion of competence
- https://wustl.edu/2010/05/highlighting-and-underlining-not-as-effective-as-previously-thought/
- https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/201302/why-you-think-you-know-it-when-you-dont

## 2. Why Venting Makes You Angrier
Most people think venting releases anger, but research shows it actually reinforces aggressive neural pathways and makes you feel worse.

Hook (en): Venting about your day is failing.
Why now: Mental health awareness is trending, and the counter-intuitive 'catharsis myth' challenges the popular advice of 'letting it all out,' driving high comment engagement.
Keywords: anger management, psychology facts, emotional intelligence, brain science, venting myths
- https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2002-01683-005
- https://news.osu.edu/venting-to-reduce-anger-isnt-effective-but-calming-activities-are/

## 3. Why Your Brain Craves Mondays
The 'Fresh Start Effect' explains why our brains use temporal landmarks to distance ourselves from past failures and boost immediate motivation.

Hook (en): Mondays are actually your superpower.
Why now: Self-improvement content performs exceptionally well on Shorts; explaining the biological urge for new beginnings provides an actionable 'aha' moment for viewers.
Keywords: motivation psychology, fresh start effect, productivity, habit building, behavioral science
- https://www.management.wharton.upenn.edu/henkel/The_Fresh_Start_Effect.pdf
- https://hbr.org/2014/08/the-fresh-start-effect
