# Video ideas: Psychology & human behavior (biases, habits, social dynamics)

## 1. Decision Fatigue: Why You Buy Junk at Night
The video reveals how the brain's willpower is a finite resource, explaining why we make poor decisions like impulse buying or snacking late in the day when our 'mental energy' is depleted.

Hook (en): Your brain has a daily battery.
Hook (fr): Ton cerveau a une batterie quotidienne.
Why now: Search trends for productivity and 'burnout' are high. This explains a universal frustration (poor evening choices) with a scientific mechanism, leading to high shareability.
Keywords: decision fatigue, psychology of willpower, impulse buying, fatigue décisionnelle, psychologie de la volonté
- Baumeister, R. F. (2002). Yielding to Temptation: Self-Regulatory Failure, Impulsive Purchasing, and Consumer Behavior. Journal of Consumer Research.

## 2. Why Your Brain Hates Good News
It explains the Negativity Bias: our brains are evolved to prioritize one bad event over ten good ones for survival, which is why we obsess over a single criticism.

Hook (en): One insult ruins your whole day.
Hook (fr): Une insulte gâche toute ta journée.
Why now: Social media comments and 'hate-watching' are current cultural peaks. Explaining why we focus on the negative provides instant relief and validation to the viewer.
Keywords: negativity bias, evolutionary psychology, mental health, biais de négativité, psychologie évolutionniste
- Baumeister, R. F., et al. (2001). Bad Is Stronger Than Good. Review of General Psychology.

## 3. Get People to Like You by Asking for a Favor
Counter-intuitively, asking someone for a small favor makes them like you more because their brain justifies the effort by assuming you are worth helping.

Hook (en): Want a friend? Ask for help.
Hook (fr): Tu veux un ami ? Demande de l'aide.
Why now: Social 'hacks' and networking tips are evergreen. This specific paradox is highly engaging because it contradicts the common fear that asking for help bothers people.
Keywords: Ben Franklin effect, cognitive dissonance, social hacks, effet Ben Franklin, dissonance cognitive
- Jecker, J., & Landy, D. (1969). Liking a Person as a Function of Doing Him a Favor. Human Relations.
