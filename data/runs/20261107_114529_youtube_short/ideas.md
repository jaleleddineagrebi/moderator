# Video ideas: Psychology & human behavior (biases, habits, social dynamics)

## 1. Why You See It Everywhere Now
The video explains the Baader-Meinhof phenomenon where your brain, after learning a new word or seeing a specific car, starts 'filtering' it into your conscious awareness, making it look like a global coincidence.

Hook (en): It’s not a coincidence.
Hook (fr): Ce n'est pas une coïncidence.
Why now: Relatable 'glitch in the matrix' experiences drive high engagement and comments. Users often experience this and think it's fate; explaining the science behind it satisfies curiosity instantly.
Keywords: Baader-Meinhof, frequency illusion, brain tricks, psychology, illusion de fréquence, cerveau, biais cognitif
- Stanford University - Arnold Zwicky (2006) 'Frequency Illusion'
- Healthline - Baader-Meinhof Phenomenon

## 2. Why Beauty Blinds Your Judgment
It reveals the Halo Effect: a cognitive bias where we automatically attribute positive traits like intelligence or kindness to attractive people without any evidence.

Hook (en): Good looks are deceiving you.
Hook (fr): La beauté te trompe.
Why now: Social dynamics and 'pretty privilege' are currently trending topics on TikTok and Reels. It challenges the viewer's own fairness, making it highly shareable.
Keywords: halo effect, first impressions, social psychology, human bias, effet de halo, psychologie sociale, jugement
- Edward Thorndike (1920) 'The Constant Error in Psychological Ratings'
- Nisbett & Wilson (1977) 'The Halo Effect'

## 3. Why You Overvalue Your Own Work
The video shows that we value products we helped create—even if they are flawed—much more than identical items built by experts, known as the IKEA Effect.

Hook (en): You overvalue things you build.
Hook (fr): Tu surestimes ce que tu crées.
Why now: Explaining why we keep 'junk' or feel proud of minor DIY tasks is a universal 'lightbulb' moment. It appeals to both the DIY community and business-minded viewers.
Keywords: IKEA effect, consumer behavior, labor and value, psychology facts, effet IKEA, comportement du consommateur, productivité
- Norton, Mochon, & Ariely (2012) 'The IKEA Effect: When Labor Leads to Love'
- Harvard Business School - The IKEA Effect
