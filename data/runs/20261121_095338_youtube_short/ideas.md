# Video ideas: Mind Hacks, AI Secrets, Bizarre Body Science & Hidden Power Dynamics

## 1. The Impossible Fist Glitch
An interactive challenge where the viewer tries to lift their ring finger while their middle finger is tucked under. It demonstrates a physical limitation of the human hand's tendons. It forces engagement as viewers fail and comment their results.

Hook (en): You can't lift this finger.
Why now: Interactive physical challenges have the highest VVSA because viewers must pause or loop the video to attempt the movement. The 'Paralyzed Ring Finger' variant is a proven viral hook format.
Keywords: brain test, human body glitch, interactive challenge, body science, hand glitch
- https://www.healthline.com/human-body-maps/extensor-digitorum-communis
- Journal of Hand Surgery: Tendon Interdependence

## 2. The Ben Franklin Request Hack
A dark psychology trick explaining why asking someone for a small favor actually makes them like you more. It leverages cognitive dissonance to flip an enemy into a friend. High comment potential for people sharing their success stories.

Hook (en): Force them to like you.
Why now: Social power dynamics are currently trending on TikTok and Shorts. Actionable 'villain' advice performs better than standard self-help due to the 'edge' factor.
Keywords: dark psychology, body language, social hacks, manipulation tricks, persuasion
- The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin
- Cognitive Dissonance Theory by Leon Festinger

## 3. The Letter F Brain Blindness
An interactive reading test showing a short sentence where the brain skip-reads the word 'OF'. It proves the brain processes 'OF' as a shape rather than a word. Viewers will rewatch to count again, driving APV over 100%.

Hook (en): Your brain is lying to you.
Why now: Visual 'blindness' tests are classic high-retention content. By challenging the viewer's intelligence, you trigger a 'verify' loop where they watch the text 3-4 times to find the missing letters.
Keywords: optical illusion, brain test, mind game, psychology test, reading glitch
- University of Cambridge: The Typoglycemia Study
- Psychology Today: Why our brains skip words
