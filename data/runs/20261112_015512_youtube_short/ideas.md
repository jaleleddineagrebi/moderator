# Video ideas: Mind Hacks, AI Secrets, Bizarre Body Science & Hidden Power Dynamics

## 1. Why Expensive Restaurants Removed Dollar Signs
Explains how high-end menus quietly delete the dollar sign ($) to trigger the 'pain of paying' reduction effect, causing diners to spend up to 8% more without noticing. Includes an on-screen interactive test where viewers compare two prices and guess which makes them hesitate, triggering comments with what their local diner charges.

Hook (en): Look closely at this menu right now.
Why now: Menu engineering and pricing psychology videos consistently generate massive debate and saves because everyday consumers immediately recognize the tactic from weekend dining. Cornell University research proves omitting currency symbols increases check totals.
Keywords: restaurant menu psychology, pain of paying, consumer mind tricks, pricing psychology, hidden money traps
- Cornell University School of Hotel Administration (Yang, Kimes, Sessarego Study on Menu Pricing Formats)
- Journal of Consumer Research on the Pain of Paying (Prelec & Loewenstein)

## 2. The Decoy Effect That Hijacks Your Wallet
Exposes why movie theaters and coffee chains introduce an absurdly priced medium option just to make the large look like a no-brainer steal. Prompts viewers with a rapid 3-second choice test that exposes their brain falling for the decoy trap, triggering viewers to comment which size they always buy.

Hook (en): You only buy large for this reason.
Why now: The asymmetric dominance effect (decoy effect) is one of the highest-retention topics in behavioral economics on short-form platforms, consistently driving high shareability and self-reflective comments from people feeling 'hacked'.
Keywords: decoy effect, pricing traps, asymmetric dominance, consumer psychology, money hacks
- Dan Ariely, 'Predictably Irrational' (The Economist Subscription Experiment)
- MIT Behavioral Economics Research on Asymmetric Dominance Effect

## 3. The Left-Digit Pricing Glitch Stealing Your Cash
Breaks down why your brain categorizes $19.99 as $10 instead of $20 due to left-digit bias, and shows how retailers exploit the millisecond your eye takes to scan left-to-right. Challenges the viewer to mentally round a receipt in two seconds, proving how effortless the brain glitch is.

Hook (en): Stop falling for 99-cent prices.
Why now: Left-digit bias is an evolutionary reading quirk that millions encounter daily in supermarkets and online shopping. Calling out the specific optical millisecond lag provides intense visual curiosity and drives viewer retention past 100%.
Keywords: charm pricing, left digit bias, retail mind tricks, psychology of money, hidden shopping tricks
- Journal of Consumer Research (Manoj Thomas and Vicki Morwitz, 'Penny Wise and Pound Foolish: The Left-Digit Effect in Price Cognition')
- Harvard Business Review on Retail Pricing Architecture
