# Video ideas: Psychology & human behavior (biases, habits, social dynamics)

## 1. Why You Feel a Phantom Phone Vibration
This video explains the sensory hallucination of phantom pocket vibrations through tactile habituation and neural misfires. It surprises viewers by revealing that their nervous system is simply over-predicting sensations due to constant device checking.

Hook (en): Your phone isn't vibrating in your pocket.
Why now: It taps into an extremely common modern sensory glitch experienced by almost every smartphone user daily, driving high initial curiosity and instant comments tagging friends.
Keywords: phantom vibration syndrome, psychology of habits, why your brain fools you, sensory hallucination
- Computers in Human Behavior journal studies on phantom vibration syndrome

## 2. Why Everyone Is Not Staring At Your Flaw
This video breaks down the psychological Spotlight Effect, demonstrating mathematically and behaviorally how little attention strangers actually pay to our minor wardrobe or social mistakes.

Hook (en): Nobody noticed your embarrassing mistake today.
Why now: Social anxiety and self-consciousness are evergreen viral topics. Offering scientific relief from embarrassment consistently drives massive saves and shares.
Keywords: spotlight effect, social anxiety psychology, cognitive bias, stop caring what people think
- Cornell University psychological studies on the spotlight effect by Gilovich, Medvec, and Savitsky

## 3. Why Too Many Options Make You Miserable
This video illustrates the Paradox of Choice, showing how adding more options to a simple decision actively decreases satisfaction and causes decision paralysis.

Hook (en): More choices actually make you less happy.
Why now: Streaming, shopping, and everyday modern life are plagued by endless options. Viewers instantly relate to feeling paralyzed by menus or Netflix screens.
Keywords: paradox of choice, decision fatigue, psychology of decision making, barry schwartz
- The Paradox of Choice by Barry Schwartz and related consumer psychology research
