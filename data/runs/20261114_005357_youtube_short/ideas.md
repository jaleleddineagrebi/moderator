# Video ideas: Mind Hacks, AI Secrets, Bizarre Body Science & Hidden Power Dynamics

## 1. The Anchoring Number Trap
An interactive test where I make you guess the price of a luxury watch after showing you a high number. It proves how stores use 'Anchoring' to make $100 look cheap by showing $1000 first. Viewers will comment their guesses to see how close they were.

Hook (en): I’m about to control your brain.
Why now: Interactive 'mind control' videos have the highest retention. The Anchoring Effect is a cornerstone of behavioral economics used by Apple and Rolex to justify high prices.
Keywords: anchoring effect, consumer psychology, marketing tricks, money traps, brain hack
- Thinking, Fast and Slow by Daniel Kahneman
- Journal of Marketing Research on Price Anchoring

## 2. The Decoy Option Trick
A live challenge showing three popcorn sizes. It explains why the 'Medium' exists only to make the 'Large' look like a steal. It forces the viewer to pick an option in their head, then reveals why they were manipulated. Comment trigger: 'What did you pick?'

Hook (en): You’re being robbed by the medium.
Why now: The Decoy Effect (Asymmetric Dominance) is currently trending in 'Dark Marketing' circles. It exploits the human inability to judge absolute value.
Keywords: decoy effect, pricing strategy, consumer behavior, billionaire secrets, spending traps
- National Geographic Brain Games: Money
- Dan Ariely's Predictably Irrational

## 3. The Sunk Cost Trap
A high-speed scenario showing why you finish a bad movie or keep a losing stock. It explains 'Loss Aversion'—why your brain hates losing $10 more than it loves winning $20. Ends with a 'Stop doing this' command to save money.

Hook (en): Stop wasting money on bad choices.
Why now: Financial 'truth-telling' content is seeing a 40% surge in engagement. Loss aversion is a primal survival mechanism that now ruins modern bank accounts.
Keywords: sunk cost fallacy, loss aversion, wealth mindset, psychology of money, financial mistakes
- The Psychology of Money by Morgan Housel
- Harvard Business Review on Sunk Cost Fallacy
