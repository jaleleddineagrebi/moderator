# Video ideas: Mind Hacks, AI Secrets, Bizarre Body Science & Hidden Power Dynamics

## 1. The Touch Trap
Explain the 'Endowment Effect' where holding an item for just 30 seconds tricks your brain into thinking you already own it, making it physically painful to put it back without buying. Includes a challenge for viewers to try it with a household object to feel the 'ownership' shift.

Hook (en): Stop touching things in the store.
Why now: This taps into a physical reflex (the Endowment Effect) that most people experience but can't name; interactive challenges like 'hold an object right now' are currently driving 100%+ retention on high-performance finance shorts.
Keywords: money traps, consumer psychology, spending hacks, endowment effect, retail secrets
- Kahneman, Knetsch, & Thaler (1990) - Experimental Tests of the Endowment Effect
- Journal of Consumer Research - The Mere Possession Effect

## 2. The Golden Triangle Menu Trick
Show a visual mockup of a menu and track the viewer's gaze to prove the 'Golden Triangle' theory: eyes move to the middle, then top right, then top left. Reveal that restaurants place their highest-profit items exactly in those spots to manipulate your choice.

Hook (en): Stop looking at the menu center.
Why now: Interactive visual tests (eye-tracking) are 'pattern interrupts' that force the viewer to stay to see if the prediction was right, leading to massive Average Percentage Viewed (APV).
Keywords: restaurant secrets, menu engineering, mind tricks, money saving hacks, consumer behavior
- Gregg Rapp - Menu Engineering Research
- Gallup Poll - Eye Tracking in Retail Environments

## 3. The 20% Mint Trick
Expose how a single free mint at the end of a meal isn't a gift, but a 'reciprocity' trigger that statistically increases tips by up to 21%. Challenge the viewer to comment if they've ever felt 'guilty' into tipping more because of a small gesture.

Hook (en): Stop eating the free restaurant mints.
Why now: Reciprocity is one of the strongest social 'power dynamics' (Cialdini); videos exposing 'free' things as traps generate high comment engagement and shares.
Keywords: psychological tricks, money secrets, tipping culture, reciprocity bias, billionaire strategy
- Strohmetz et al. (2002) - Give and ye shall receive: The effect of giving a small gift on tipping
- Robert Cialdini - Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion
