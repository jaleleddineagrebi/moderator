# Video ideas: Psychology & human behavior (biases, habits, social dynamics)

## 1. The Favor Trick to Make Friends
Explain why asking someone for a small favor actually makes them like you more, reversing the common logic that we should do things for others to be liked.

Hook (en): Stop doing favors to be liked.
Hook (fr): Arrête de rendre service pour plaire.
Why now: Social engineering and 'dark' psychology hacks are currently peaking on TikTok and Shorts. This specific counter-intuitive advice generates high comments from skeptics.
Keywords: psychology hacks, social dynamics, Benjamin Franklin effect, friendship tips, persuasion, psychologie, astuce sociale
- Jecker & Landy (1969) study on the Benjamin Franklin Effect
- The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin

## 2. Why You See the Same Thing Everywhere
Explore the Frequency Illusion: why after you learn a new word or see a new car, it suddenly appears everywhere in your daily life.

Hook (en): You're seeing this everywhere for a reason.
Hook (fr): Tu vois ça partout pour une raison.
Why now: This 'glitch in the matrix' style content has high retention because it explains a common eerie experience viewers have all felt but couldn't name.
Keywords: brain facts, frequency illusion, cognitive bias, perception, Baader-Meinhof, cerveau, psychologie
- Stanford University Linguistics (Arnold Zwicky, 2005)
- Selective Attention and Confirmation Bias research

## 3. Why Being Clumsy is Your Superpower
Explain the Pratfall Effect: how making a small mistake or being slightly clumsy makes highly competent people more attractive and relatable to others.

Hook (en): Your mistakes make you more likable.
Hook (fr): Tes erreurs te rendent plus attirant.
Why now: Vulnerability is a trending topic in self-improvement. It provides relief to viewers who feel the pressure of perfectionism, making it highly shareable.
Keywords: likability, social psychology, clumsiness, attraction, Pratfall Effect, charisme, relations humaines
- Elliot Aronson (1966) 'The influence of a spill on the evaluation of a person'
- Social Psychology (Myers & Twenge)
